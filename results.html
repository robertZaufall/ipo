Which buying method gets the best price?

All numbers = how much above the lowest possible price each method paid. Lower is better. The recommended ladder wins; the machine-learning model and the auto-optimizer don't beat it.

"Buy at preset times" = the best fixed clock times. "Auto-optimizer" rows show what happens if you let an algorithm re-tune the orders for the selected company-size filter — shown only as a sanity check, because that tends to fool itself on small samples. "ML model" = a machine-learning classifier that tries to spot good moments minute by minute.

Does it work on IPOs it has never seen? the honest test

How this was tested: each strategy only ever saw IPOs from the past and was then judged on the 79 newest IPOs it had never seen. Lower numbers = bought closer to the low.

  • Static ladder — the recommended recipe from above, fixed once and never re-tuned. It beats every strategy that kept re-optimizing itself.
  • Adaptive policy — watches the first 30 minutes, then either buys immediately or places deep limit orders. Slightly better on day 1, especially in bad cases.
  • Don't expect much below ~7%. Even a strategy allowed to cheat and look at the test IPOs in advance only reaches 6.7%, and the average dollar actually traded in the market paid 7.8% above the low. The recommended ladder buys cheaper than the average market participant — the rest of the gap to the exact low is luck, not method.
  • The data behind this is checked: all 157 IPOs passed an integrity audit, day-1 prices match an independent second source, and the order-execution logic is covered by unit tests.

Can machine learning do better?

Short answer: no. A model trained on minute-by-minute trading data finds real patterns, but its buying results are worse than the simple bargain-order plan — so the recommendation stays simple.
